Set up Slouchpad

Slouchpad needs its free companion app, Slouchpad for Mac, on your Mac. Three steps: put it on your Mac, let it control the pointer and keys, then scan its QR code with your iPhone.

DownloadSlouchpad for Mac

Open this page on your Mac. macOS 13 or later. Don't have the iPhone app yet? Get it from the App Store.

  1. Install Slouchpad for Mac

    Download Slouchpad for Mac, drag it to Applications and open it. It lives in your menu bar behind a couch icon.

  2. Permit

    On your Mac: Under Privacy & Security, allow Slouchpad in Accessibility so it can move the pointer and press keys for your phone. The button in the menu takes you there.

    On your iPhone: When it asks to find devices on your local network, tap Allow, so it can find your Mac. If you tapped Don’t Allow, switch Local Network on in Settings, then Slouchpad.

  3. Connect

    Click the Slouchpad icon in your Mac’s menu bar to see its QR code. In the iPhone app, tap Scan QR Code and point the camera at it (the first time, allow camera access). Your iPhone connects, and does so again by itself while the app is open. When you close the app it forgets your Mac, so you scan the code again next time. You can also scan the code with the iPhone’s Camera app. If you can’t scan, choose Copy link instead in the menu, send the link to your iPhone in a message and open it there.

If your iPhone can’t find your Mac

Some guest, hotel and coworking Wi-Fi networks stop devices finding each other. Slouchpad then connects directly between the iPhone and the Mac, which works when they are close together and Wi-Fi is switched on on both (they don’t need to be on the same network). The pointer can be a little less smooth that way. If it still can’t connect, check that Slouchpad is running on the Mac (look for its icon in the menu bar), that your iPhone has Local Network access, and that no VPN or firewall is in the way.

Your iPhone and Mac are best on the same Wi-Fi network. Brightness works on the screens macOS lets apps control, such as the built-in display and Apple displays. Many other monitors can only be adjusted with their own buttons.
