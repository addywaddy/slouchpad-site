Set up Slouchpad

Slouchpad needs its free companion app, Slouchpad for Mac, on your Mac. Four steps: put the app on your iPhone and Slouchpad for Mac on your Mac, let it control the pointer and keys, then scan its QR code with your iPhone.

Using Slouchpad 1.1 or earlier, with a PIN? See the 1.1 guide.

Open this page on your Mac. macOS 13 or later.

  1. Install Slouchpad on your iPhone

    On your iPhone, open the App Store and search for Slouchpad, or open this link on your iPhone. If you came here from the link in the app, you already have it: go on to step 2.

  2. Install Slouchpad for Mac

    Slouchpad for Mac, the companion application to Slouchpad for iPhone, lives in the menu bar at the top of your screen, behind a couch icon .

    DownloadSlouchpad for Mac
    1. Open the Downloads folder in Finder.
    2. Double-click the file Slouchpad.dmg.
    3. Drag the Slouchpad icon onto the Applications folder next to it.
    4. Open the Applications folder in Finder and double-click Slouchpad. If macOS asks whether you are sure you want to open an app downloaded from the internet, click Open. (If you open Slouchpad from anywhere else, it offers to move itself to Applications. Say yes.)
    5. If macOS asks whether Slouchpad may find devices on local networks (newer versions of macOS do), click Allow, so that your iPhone can reach your Mac. If you clicked Don’t Allow, you can switch it on in the next step.
    6. Optional: eject the disk image by clicking the eject button next to Slouchpad in the Finder sidebar.

  3. Allow Slouchpad to move the pointer and press keys

    Slouchpad needs two settings in Privacy & Security: control of your Mac’s pointer and keys, so that your iPhone can move and type, and Local Network, so that your iPhone can find your Mac.

    1. Look for the couch icon in the menu bar, at the top right of your screen, and click it to open Slouchpad’s menu.
    2. The card at the top of the menu lists the settings that are still off. Click its Open Privacy & Security Settings button.
    3. In the list that opens, choose Accessibility (called Device Control and Data Access on the newest macOS), then turn on the switch next to Slouchpad. macOS may ask for your password or Touch ID.
    4. Go back and choose Local Network (newer versions of macOS only), and turn on the switch next to Slouchpad.

    Each setting gets a tick in the card as it is switched on, and the menu then shows All set and the QR code for the next step. If you can’t see the card, open System Settings yourself and go to Privacy & Security.

  4. Connect

    1. Open Slouchpad on your iPhone. When it asks to find devices on your local network, tap Allow.
    2. Tap Scan QR Code. The first time, tap OK to let Slouchpad use the camera, which it only uses to read the code.
    3. Hold your iPhone up so that it can see the QR code in Slouchpad’s menu on your Mac. Click the couch icon if the menu isn’t open.
    4. You are connected when the menu on your Mac shows a green dot and Connected: iPhone, and your iPhone shows its remote controls.

    You can also scan the code with the iPhone’s Camera app. If you can’t scan, click Copy link instead in the Mac’s menu, send the link to your iPhone in a message and open it there.

    While the app is open, your iPhone reconnects to your Mac by itself. When you close the app it forgets your Mac, so you scan the code again next time. If you tapped Don’t Allow for the local network or the camera, open the iPhone’s Settings, choose Slouchpad and switch them on.

Your iPhone and Mac work best on the same Wi-Fi network. If the Slouchpad menu on your Mac says Over the direct Wi-Fi link, the pointer may be less smooth; join both devices to the same network. Brightness works on the screens macOS lets apps control, such as the built-in display and Apple displays. Many other monitors can only be adjusted with their own buttons.

Can’t find your Mac, or something else not working? See Support, or write to support@slouchpad.com.
