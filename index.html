slouchpad

Your Mac, from the couch.

Slouchpad turns your iPhone into a media remote, trackpad and keyboard for controlling your Mac. Play, pause, skip, type, change the volume and dim the screen from the comfort of your couch.

DownloadSlouchpad for Mac

Features

Remote

Everything you need to watch video on your Mac without leaving the couch.

  • Play/pause, skip backward and forward
  • Volume and brightness controls, with output selection
  • Fullscreen mode

Trackpad

A plain glass trackpad for your Mac.

  • Move, click, scroll and drag
  • Tap with three fingers to go back to the remote
  • Pointer speed and visual trail controls (see Settings > Controls)

Keyboard

Type with your iPhone's keyboard and the text appears on your Mac, in whichever app is in front.

  • Input sent in real time to your Mac
  • Large preview of entered text on the Mac (see Settings > Controls)
  • Native audio transcription

Apps

Your Mac's Dock, on your phone. Switch apps without reaching for the Mac.

  • Apps appear in the Dock's own order, with closed ones dimmed
  • Tap to bring an app to the front, or to open it; tap the front app again to step through its windows
  • Hold an app to quit it, or to send its window to another screen
  • Favourites: switch to it at the top to open the apps and websites you chose on your Mac, such as Netflix or YouTube, with one tap. Hold one to open it on another screen

Settings

Everything is in three tabs: Connection, Controls and Appearance.

Connection

Pair with your Mac by scanning its QR code.

  • Scan QR Code: point your iPhone at the code in the Slouchpad menu on your Mac. It connects, and does so again by itself while the app is open. Closing the app forgets your Mac
  • Disconnect: forgets the Mac, as closing the app does. Scan its code to connect again, or scan another Mac’s code to switch
  • Step by step, see the setup guide

Controls

How the pointer and the Mac respond.

  • Pointer speed and pointer colour
  • Pointer trail on Mac: a fading trail follows the pointer, so it's easy to find from the couch
  • Magnifier on Mac: zooms in around the pointer while the trackpad or the typing screen is showing. It needs “Use keyboard shortcuts to zoom” switched on in System Settings, Accessibility, Zoom on your Mac
  • Typing preview on Mac: shows what you type in large letters on the Mac
  • Share crash reports: off until you switch it on. It sends an anonymous report if Slouchpad crashes, and nothing else

Appearance

Easy on the eyes when the lights are down.

  • Dark mode: a black background with a colour you choose
  • Brightness: dim the app below your iPhone's own minimum brightness
  • Colour: pick the accent from the rainbow slider

Connecting

My iPhone can't find my Mac
  • Check that both are on the same Wi-Fi network. Guest networks, some office networks and some hotel or student Wi-Fi stop devices seeing each other.
  • If your iPhone still can't see the Mac, bring the two close together and make sure Wi-Fi is switched on on both. Slouchpad then connects directly, without the network. The pointer can be a little less smooth that way.
  • Check the Slouchpad sofa icon is in your Mac's menu bar. If not, open Slouchpad from Applications.
  • On your iPhone, open Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network and make sure Slouchpad is on.
  • If you use a VPN on either device, try turning it off.
  • If your Mac's firewall is on, allow incoming connections for Slouchpad in System Settings → Network → Firewall.
  • The app says what it is waiting for, and offers Try again after a failure. If it says Local Network access is off, tap Open Settings and switch it on.
Where do I find the QR code?

Click the Slouchpad icon in your Mac's menu bar. The QR code is shown there. In the iPhone app tap Scan QR Code and point the camera at it, or open the iPhone's Camera app and point it at the code. If you can't scan, choose Copy link instead in the menu, send the link to your iPhone in a message and open it there.

It says my Mac has a new code

The code changes when you choose New code in the Slouchpad menu, and the old one stops working. Scan the new one. The iPhone also asks you to scan again if you reinstalled Slouchpad on the Mac, because the Mac then has a new security certificate.

It worked yesterday and now it won't connect

The Mac may be asleep, switched off, or on a different network, or Slouchpad may not be running on it. Open the iPhone app: it says what it is waiting for, and connects by itself as soon as your Mac is in reach. If you closed the app, it has forgotten your Mac, so scan the code again. If it says the Mac has a new code or a different certificate, scan the code again.

Can I use more than one Mac?

The iPhone is paired with one Mac at a time. To switch, scan the other Mac's code. Disconnect on the Connection tab forgets the Mac, and you scan its code to connect again.

Is the connection secure?

Yes. Everything between your iPhone and your Mac, including what you type, is encrypted. Your iPhone only connects to the Mac whose code you scanned, and only a phone that has scanned the code can connect. If anyone could see your Mac's screen they could see the code, so choose New code in the menu if that matters.

What do I need to use Slouchpad?

An iPhone with iOS 16 or later, and a Mac with macOS 13 or later running the free Slouchpad for Mac. Both need to be on the same Wi-Fi network.

Using it

The pointer doesn't move, or clicks and keys do nothing

Slouchpad needs the Accessibility permission to act as a mouse and keyboard. Until it has it, the iPhone app shows One more step. Click the Slouchpad icon in your Mac's menu bar: its button takes you to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, where you switch Slouchpad on. The iPhone app carries on by itself within a few seconds.

How do I type on my Mac?

Tap the keyboard icon in the bar along the bottom of the app. Whatever you type appears on your Mac as you type, in the app that's in front, so click the search box or text field on your Mac first (with the trackpad). Drag along the space bar to move the cursor; the Mac's cursor follows. The four arrow keys above the keyboard also move the cursor, including into text that was already there, and Backspace deletes it. The Return key presses Return on the Mac, for example to submit a search.

The text I type shows large on my Mac. Can I turn that off?

Yes. In the iPhone app go to Settings → Controls and switch off Typing preview on Mac. The preview is also hidden automatically while a password field is selected.

How do I get back from the trackpad or typing screen?

Tap any icon in the bar along the bottom, or tap the trackpad with three fingers to go back to the remote.

What is the pointer trail on my Mac's screen?

While you use the trackpad, your Mac can show a thin fading trail behind the pointer, and a soft ring that pings outwards for a couple of seconds where it stops. Both help you find the pointer from across the room. Switch it off in Settings → Controls with Pointer trail on Mac, or choose its colour with the Pointer colour strip: drag all the way left for white, or along the rainbow for a colour.

How do I use it in a dark room?

In Settings → Appearance turn on Dark mode. Then use the Brightness strip to dim the app further than the iPhone's own screen allows. Then use the Colour strip: drag it all the way left for white, or along the rainbow for a colour. Warm colours such as orange and red are the gentlest on your eyes in the dark.

How do I drag a window?

Put the pointer on the window's title bar, then hold one finger still on the trackpad until you feel a tap. Slide your finger to move the window and lift it to let go.

The brightness slider is missing or does nothing

macOS only lets apps change brightness on some screens, such as the built-in display and Apple displays. Many other monitors can only be adjusted with their own buttons or on-screen menu. The slider appears only for screens Slouchpad can control.

How does the fullscreen switch work?

In the Apple TV, Photos and QuickTime Player apps it sends fn + F, the Mac's fullscreen shortcut. In a browser it presses F to make the video fullscreen and Esc to leave it, which is what video players on streaming sites listen for. The switch follows the window's real state, including when you change it on the Mac.

Does it work away from home?

Slouchpad works over a local Wi-Fi network only. Both devices have to be on the same network. It doesn't connect over the internet, which is also why nothing leaves your network.

Can I try it without a Mac?

No. Slouchpad is a remote for your Mac and needs the free Slouchpad for Mac app to do anything. Get it from the setup guide.

What does the Apps screen do?

It shows the apps in your Mac's Dock, in the Dock's own order, with closed ones dimmed, followed by your other open apps. Tap an app to bring it to the front, or to open it if it is closed. Tap the app that is already in front to step through its windows. Hold an app to quit it, or to send its window to another screen when your Mac has more than one.

What are Favourites?

A second view on the Apps screen: switch between Apps and Favourites at the top. Favourites holds the apps and websites you chose on your Mac, for the things you start often, such as Netflix, Max, Prime Video or YouTube. Tap one to open it. A website opens in your Mac's default browser.

How do I add favourites?

On your Mac, click the Slouchpad icon in the menu bar and choose Favourites…. In the window that opens, choose Add App… to pick an app, or Add Website and type a name and the address, such as netflix.com. Drag the rows to change the order, and use the minus button to remove one. The iPhone updates by itself.

How do I open a favourite on another screen?

Hold it on the iPhone and choose the screen, such as your TV. The Mac opens the app or website and moves its window to the middle of that screen. It needs the Accessibility permission and a Mac with more than one screen that isn't mirroring them. A website moves the browser's window, tabs included. If the window can't be moved, the iPhone says so.

A website in Favourites has no icon

When you add a website, your Mac downloads its icon once from the site. Some sites don't offer one, or block that kind of request, and then the tile shows a globe. In the Favourites window, the picture button at the end of the row lets you choose a picture of your own, or try the website's icon again.

The Apps screen says to update Slouchpad on my Mac

Your Mac is running an older version of Slouchpad that can't list its apps. Download the latest Slouchpad for Mac from the top of this page, open it and the Apps screen will fill in.

A window won't move to my other screen

Moving a window needs the Accessibility permission (see above) and a Mac with more than one screen that isn't mirroring them. Some apps limit where their windows can go, and Slouchpad can't move those.

How do I use the Magnifier?

In the iPhone app go to Settings → Controls and switch on Magnifier on Mac. While the trackpad or the typing screen is showing, your Mac's Zoom follows the pointer. It needs Use keyboard shortcuts to zoom switched on in System Settings → Accessibility → Zoom on your Mac.

What are crash reports?

An optional switch, off until you turn it on, that sends us an anonymous report when Slouchpad crashes or freezes. It sends nothing about what you do or type. It is in Settings → Controls on the iPhone and in the Slouchpad menu on the Mac. See the privacy policy for what a report contains.

Can Slouchpad start when I log in to my Mac?

Yes. Switch on Open at login in the Slouchpad menu on your Mac. macOS may ask you to confirm it in System Settings.

Removing Slouchpad

How do I uninstall it?
  1. Choose Quit Slouchpad from the menu bar icon.
  2. Drag Slouchpad from Applications to the Bin.
  3. Optionally remove it from System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.
  4. Delete the app from your iPhone as usual.

Still stuck?

Write to support@slouchpad.com and tell us your macOS and iOS versions, the version of the Mac and iPhone apps if you know them, and what you see.

Privacy policy

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Slouchpad does not collect, share or sell any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code that tracks you. The one thing it can send us is an anonymous crash report, and only if you switch that on (see Crash reports below).

How it works

Slouchpad has two parts: an iPhone app and a free Mac app called Slouchpad for Mac. They talk to each other directly, over your own Wi-Fi network or a direct link between the two devices, and the connection is encrypted. Nothing you do in Slouchpad, such as pointer movements, key presses or media controls, leaves your network or passes through any server of ours.

What stays on your devices

  • On your iPhone: nothing about your Mac is saved. The name of the Mac you paired with, its pairing key and the fingerprint of its security certificate (all from the QR code you scanned) are kept in the app’s memory only, and are gone when you close the app. Your settings (dark mode, colour, brightness, pointer speed, pointer colour, whether to show the pointer trail on the Mac, whether to use the magnifier, and whether to share crash reports).
  • On your Mac: the pairing key shown in the QR code, the security certificate the Mac made for itself (in your keychain), your Favourites list (the apps and websites you chose, with the icons of the websites), whether the app opens at login, and whether to share crash reports.

This information is kept only on those devices and is removed when you delete the apps.

Typing

When you use the typing screen, the text you type is sent straight to your Mac, encrypted, and typed into the app you are using there. Unless you switch it off in Settings, the Mac also shows the text in large type on its screen for a moment. The preview is hidden while a password field is selected. Slouchpad does not keep or send anywhere what you type.

Your Mac’s apps

The Apps screen shows the apps kept in your Mac’s Dock and the other apps that are open. To do that, your Mac sends their names and icons to your iPhone over your Wi-Fi network, while you use the app. The iPhone keeps them in the app’s memory only, never saves them to its storage, and does not send them anywhere else. Slouchpad does not see what is inside those apps.

Favourites

The Favourites view shows the apps and websites you chose in the Favourites window on your Mac. The Mac sends the list, and the icons of its apps, to your iPhone over your Wi-Fi network or the direct link while you use the app; the iPhone keeps them in the app’s memory only. The iPhone never sends a web address: it asks the Mac to open an entry of your list by its number. To show a website’s icon, the Mac downloads it once from that website when you add it, like any web request, so the website sees the Mac’s IP address, and the icon is kept on your Mac. Slouchpad sends nothing about this to us or to anyone else.

Crash reports

Crash reports are off by default. If you switch on Share crash reports (in Settings on the iPhone, or in the Slouchpad menu on the Mac), the app sends a report to us when it crashes or freezes, so that we can fix the problem. You can switch it off again at any time; reporting stops at once.

A report contains the technical details of the problem: the app version, the iPhone or Mac model and system version, the language and time zone, how much memory was free, basic device state such as battery level and screen size, and the code that was running. It does not contain what you typed, the name of your Mac, your PIN, your settings, screenshots or a list of what you did in the app. It carries a random identifier made for the app on that device, which cannot be linked to you.

Reports are processed by Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.), on servers in the European Union, and we keep them for 90 days. Sentry sees the connection’s IP address when it receives a report, so we have asked it not to store it.

Permissions

  • Local Network (iPhone): lets the app find and connect to your Mac on your Wi-Fi network.
  • Camera (iPhone): used only when you tap Scan QR Code, to read the code in your Mac’s menu. The picture is not recorded, saved or sent anywhere.
  • Accessibility (Mac): lets Slouchpad for Mac move the pointer and press keys when you ask it to from your phone.

Slouchpad for Mac does not read the contents of your screen, your keystrokes or your files. Slouchpad does not use the microphone, location, contacts or photos, and uses the camera only to scan that code.
